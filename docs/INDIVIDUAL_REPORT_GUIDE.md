# Individual report guide

Course requirement: each member submits an individual PDF with at least **600 words**, explaining their own contributions. This guide is not a completed report. A Chinese character count is not automatically an English word count; follow the instructor's language and counting rules. If writing in English, aim for approximately 750–900 words to leave margin.

| Section | Suggested English words | Write from actual evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | 70–90 | Your role, project goal, and what this report will explain |
| Project description | 100–120 | Target users, CLI/browser workflow and scope |
| Personal contributions | 250–300 | Specific files/functions/design decisions/tests you actually worked on; team collaboration; distinguish inherited/AI-generated work from your changes |
| Challenges faced | 120–150 | One or two real bugs or tradeoffs, reproduction, alternatives, resolution and test evidence |
| Lessons learned | 100–130 | Python concepts learned, review/testing skills, AI verification, what you would change |
| Conclusion | 60–80 | What your work achieved and remaining limitations |

Possible evidence: dated notes, repository changes, test outputs, peer review records, demo preparation and screenshots. These are examples of evidence to gather, not facts to claim without doing the work.

Before exporting: verify at least 600 words; include your real identity where required; cite external code/resources and AI collaboration; remove all placeholders; check the PDF visually. Do not submit another member's report or a fabricated contribution narrative.
